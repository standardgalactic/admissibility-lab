## Proposed abstract

Computational expenditure can serve several purposes at once. A machine may perform an informationally unhelpful task while producing heat that meets a real human need, and a valuable computation may produce heat at a place or time where nobody can use it. These possibilities complicate any argument that replaces waste-based verification with usefulness-based trust. Bringing an account of informational security into contact with a proposal for ecologically integrated computation, this essay distinguishes the usefulness of a physical byproduct from the evidential relevance of the process that produced it. It asks whether infrastructure can improve its ecological performance without improving its warrant for admitting identities, validating claims, or allocating authority. The proposed argument is that ecological compatibility and epistemic warrant require separately specified obligations, even when one installation satisfies both. A worked heating-and-verification case examines how useful thermal production could coexist with duplicate identities, fabricated contributions, or incentive-driven expansion of unnecessary computation. The conclusion would establish a design distinction rather than a general impossibility result: useful heat may justify an infrastructural activity under some conditions, while the right to count that activity as evidence requires an additional, task-specific argument. The essay's empirical and security premises remain questions for investigation.

## The bridge

A relocates trust from arbitrary expenditure toward meaningful contribution. B proposes making computational expenditure contribute to human and ecological needs. Reading these proposals together raises a new question: does making expenditure useful repair its role as a credential, or only repair one of its externalities? The proposed essay needs A to define the verification problem and B to supply a case in which physical expenditure ceases to be straightforwardly wasteful.

## Expenditure with two beneficiaries

Begin with a hypothetical computer heating an occupied building while participating in a digital verification system. State the two services independently: thermal demand satisfied and a verification decision made. The case should not presume that all heating is beneficial, or that all computations performed during heating are necessary. This opening turns the general dispute over waste into a question about what an expenditure is evidence of.

## Useful to whom, for which obligation

Introduce a distinction between service contribution and evidential warrant. Heat can contribute to a building's thermal service; an admission mechanism must address a specified attack or uncertainty. The new conjecture is that success on the former obligation does not, by itself, answer the latter. Develop this through an explicit identity-verification example rather than treating the distinction as an established theorem.

## The counterexample of productive fabrication

Construct a hypothetical operator who generates fabricated submissions while recovering the resulting heat. If useful heat alone counted as warrant for informational admission, the submissions could qualify despite their fabrication. This challenges an inference from ecological usefulness to epistemic legitimacy, rather than challenging heat recovery itself. An actual system may already have separate validity checks, in which case the counterexample identifies their indispensable role.

## The limits of informational scoring

Return the objection to A. Its proposed scalar usefulness score also needs a measurement procedure, a threat model, and a defense against imitation or manipulation. A score that rewards density or coherence might favor convincing fabrication unless its relation to the relevant obligation is established. The synthesis therefore constrains both sources: thermal usefulness and semantic usefulness each need independent warrant for their use in security decisions.

## An installation with separately testable obligations

Sketch a design with independently assessed heat demand, contribution validity, duplicate-admission resistance, and energy consumption. These obligations may interact without collapsing into one measure. A scheduling rule could favor valid work during useful heating periods, but admission would still depend on the verification rule. This is an engineering proposal requiring evaluation, not a claim that a suitable verifier has already been constructed.

## A discriminating study and the strongest objection

The strongest objection is that proof of expenditure can have a security purpose regardless of whether its byproduct is useful, so the proposed essay may attack an inference that neither source endorses. Meet that objection by stating a narrow target: the unsupported substitution of service usefulness for a specified security guarantee. A study would compare a fixed verification task under matched attack budgets, with and without useful heat recovery, recording false admissions, honest-user exclusion, total energy, and displaced heating demand. If security performance remains unchanged while net heating costs improve, the ecological benefit is real but distinct from increased warrant. If system incentives induce additional unnecessary work or increase attack capacity, the interaction requires a fuller model. Either outcome would sharpen the proposed distinction without establishing a universal result about infrastructure or trust.
